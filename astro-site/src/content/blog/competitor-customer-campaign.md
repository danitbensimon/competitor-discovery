## Results at InfiniGrow

I ran this campaign at InfiniGrow, a B2B SaaS company that sold to Fortune 500 enterprises and was later acquired. We sent outbound emails to our competitor's customers, with no ads or calls in the first wave.

| Metric | Cold lists | Competitor customers |
|---|---|---|
| List to meeting conversion | 0.01% | **1%** |
| Cold to opportunity | Baseline | **10x** |
| Biggest deal | n/a | **Largest ACV in company history** |

Cold lists convert at about 1 meeting per 10,000 contacts. Tweaking Google Ads bids saves a few dollars, but it won't move that number. Changing who's on the list moved it 100x for us.

## Why competitor customers convert

- **About 5% of your market is buying right now.** Most demand gen chases the other 95%.
- **They already know the category.** You skip the education stage, because they bought a tool like yours once.
- **They have a reason to leave.** Their complaints are public, in reviews and on calls with your sales team.

I turned that campaign into a system I call SPY, with 3 letters for 3 steps.

## SPY at a glance

| Letter | Step | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Signs | 1 competitor worth targeting |
| **P** | Pull | Its customers and their buying committees |
| **Y** | Yank them over | A first message built on their complaint |

## S. Signs to pick a competitor

Spy on 4 signs before you pick, and choose the competitor that shows up in 4 of 4.

- **CRM.** Its name keeps showing up in Closed Lost and Closed Won.
- **Reviews.** The same complaint repeats in its G2 reviews.
- **Sales calls.** Prospects bring it up and complain about the same thing.
- **Market.** After an acquisition or a price hike, its customers start looking around.

Write down the complaint that repeats across the 4. That complaint becomes your opening line in step Y.

## P. Pull customers and buying committees

1. **Pull the customer list.** Look in case studies, job posts, tech stack data and review sites. [competitorcustomer.com](/) does it for free and links the source behind a company.
2. **Keep the companies with proof.** A case study or a job post naming the tool beats a tech stack database hit.
3. **Name the buying committee.** Find who signs the contract and who uses the tool. I enrich them in Clay.
4. **Score them against your ICP.** Send the best fits to your SDRs or to a high-touch sequence, and the rest to an automated one.

## Y. Yank them over

Open with the complaint you wrote down in step S. Here's the line I use.

> Did [Competitor] raise your renewal price again? Here's what switching looks like.

Then make leaving easy.

- **Offer to buy out the rest of their contract.** It removes the reason to wait for renewal.
- **Ask for 15 minutes.** A short ask fits a contact who's already annoyed with their vendor.
- **Stay specific.** Name the competitor and the complaint. Skip the generic pitch about your platform.

## Campaign from A to Z

| Stage | Action |
|---|---|
| Pick | Check the 4 signs and choose 1 competitor |
| Pull | Build the customer list with a linked source per company |
| Enrich | Add the buying committee and score against your ICP |
| Write | Open with the repeated complaint and the contract buyout |
| Send | Email first; SDRs call the best-fit accounts |
| Measure | Meetings booked from this list vs your cold lists |
